About

Why should we trust
the model's outputs?

My research trajectory was transformed during a visiting scholarship at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center. When presenting deep learning results for MRI reconstruction to the principal investigator, a medical physicist, he asked not about the quality of the results, but about why we should trust the model's outputs.

"How do I know this isn't hallucinating?"

This question shifted my focus from input-output performance to the internal mechanisms of AI. For AI systems to be truly accountable in high-stakes domains, we must understand their internal processes.

My work focuses on three areas: mitigating harmful content generation, mechanistic interpretability of large language models, and machine unlearning for privacy.

Research Agenda

Building trust through
mechanistic understanding

01

Mechanistic Interpretability for AI Safety

Developing methodology for fine-grained backdoor circuit extraction in LLMs. Using backdoor attacks as a strategic starting point because their trigger-response patterns allow for rigorous validation of interpretability tools. Extending to identify circuits responsible for emergent social biases, refusal bypass vulnerabilities, and hallucination patterns through specific neurons and attention heads.

02

Mechanistic Privacy Preservation in Medical Data

Investigating de-identification techniques using mechanistic interpretability methods such as Sparse Autoencoders and Transcoders. Proposing a framework that identifies and manipulates neurons responsible for processing sensitive entities within an LLM-based architecture, ensuring both text-based explainability and mechanistic interpretability.

03

Safety Evaluation Frameworks for Generative AI

Developing a dual-aspect evaluation strategy integrating black-box testing with white-box diagnostics. Building dynamic benchmarks using automated red-teaming for adversarial robustness, and an internal diagnostic paradigm that inspects what a model contains rather than just what it outputs.
